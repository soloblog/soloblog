# Creating pages

A page is written like a post, in the same editor. This part of the
guide shows how to make one look like what it is - a recipe, a price list,
an About page in your menu, or the front page of your site.

In the editor, the post's settings open from **Settings**, the gear icon
beside **Save**. The steps below call that panel **Post settings**,
which is the name at its top.

## Make a recipe, resume or other kind of page

Start from a template for the kind of page you want. It gives you the page
with its sections, sample words and settings already in place, so you write
over them instead of building the page yourself.

1. Select **New post**, then **Template** - the label with ▾ under the
   title.
2. Choose the template for your kind of page. **Which template?**, below,
   says which.
3. Replace the sample words with your own. To drop a part you don't need,
   delete its `##` heading and everything under it, up to the next `##`.
4. Open **Post settings > Publishing**, switch **Published** on and select
   **Save**.

Each part of the page is a [section](#add-sections) with its own look, and
the template has already chosen the page's settings - its title, its width
and what shows around it. Change either whenever you like: the page is
yours, and the template stays as it was.

**Which template?**

- **A recipe** - **Recipe**: the times and servings, ingredients to check off
  off, and numbered steps. Readers get a **Print** button at the top, and
  search engines understand it as a recipe.
- **A resume** - **Resume / CV**: jobs and schooling with their dates, and
  a list of skills. Print is on, and the byline, likes and share buttons
  are off.
- **An interview** - **Interview**: questions and answers as a chat. Start
  each paragraph with the speaker in bold, like `**Sam:**`.
- **A personal homepage or About page** - **Profile / home**: your photo, a
  big line about you, a short bio, buttons to your links and your latest
  posts. The page's own title is hidden.
- **A landing page** - **Landing page**: see
  [Build a landing page](#build-a-landing-page).
- **A project or a story in images** - **Project** or **Photo story**: see
  [Show work as a portfolio](#show-work-as-a-portfolio).
- **A shop, studio or practice** - **Small business**: buttons to call,
  write or find you, and your opening hours. Search engines understand it
  as a local business.
- **A media kit** - **Media kit / press**: bios with a **Copy** button each,
  and photos with **Download** links, for journalists and podcast hosts.
- **A page before launch** - **Coming soon**: a centred title, a line about
  what's coming and a sign-up box. Nothing else shows around it.
- **A page that's mostly one thing** - **Book or film reviews**, **FAQ**,
  **Milestones**, **Pricing**, **Services**, **Testimonials**, **Talks and
  events**, **Menu**, **Team**, **Shop**, **Uses** and **Portfolio** each
  start with that one section, ready to fill in.
- **Slides, a now page, a wiki note, a link post or a guide** - **Slides**,
  **Now page**, **Wiki / notes**, **Link post** and **Docs / guide** each
  set the matching Post setting for you. See
  [Show a page as slides](#show-a-page-as-slides),
  [Keep a now page up to date](#keep-a-now-page-up-to-date),
  [Build a wiki](#build-a-wiki),
  [Add a link or a built-in page to the menu](#add-a-link-or-a-built-in-page-to-the-menu)
  and [Start a series](#start-a-series).

**Already wrote the page?** Open **Post settings > Template** and select
**Apply a template** - see
[Apply a template to a page](writing.md#apply-a-template-to-a-page).

**Want every template, and what each one sets?** See
[Templates](sections-templates.md#templates).

## Show work as a portfolio

Make a portfolio: one page with your work, images first, and a page for
each project, laid out like a magazine. It suits anyone whose work is seen -
a photographer, a designer, an illustrator, a maker.

1. Write a page for each project. Select **New post**, then **Template**,
   and choose **Project**. It opens with a strip of facts, a story laid
   out like a magazine, and a link to the next project. Give it a **Cover
   image** (**Post settings > Details**) - the project opens on it, full
   screen.
2. Under **At a glance**, replace the sample lines with the project's own,
   like `Client: Corner Café` and `Year: 2026`. They show at the top as
   its facts.
3. Write the story under the heading **The story**, and put your images
   between the paragraphs. The page places them for you - see
   [Photo story](sections-templates.md#section-photo-story).
4. Give each project the same tag, such as `work` - in **Post settings >
   Details**, under **Tags**.
5. Make one more page for the portfolio itself: select **New post**, then
   **Template**, and choose **Portfolio**. Under its `Section: Portfolio`
   line, change `Tag: Default` to `Tag: work`, delete the three sample
   pieces under it (each a `###` heading), and write a line or two about
   what you make.
6. Select **Save** on each page.

The portfolio shows each project by its cover, title and summary, newest
first. Each project ends with a large link to the next one with the same
first tag.

**Want to choose what's in it and in what order?** Leave `Tag:` as
`Default` and keep the `###` pieces instead, each with its image, a line
about it, and a link to its page. A piece without a link opens its image
bigger. With both, the pieces you wrote come first, then the tagged pages.

**Want another arrangement?** Go to **Settings > Posts**, and under
**Images** choose **Portfolio pages**. For one portfolio only, change the
`Show as:` line under its `Section:` line, like `Show as: Rows` - the five
arrangements are listed in
[Portfolio](sections-templates.md#section-portfolio).

**A story in images, not a project?** Start from **Photo story** instead:
the same magazine layout without the facts. Travel photos, a day out, a
making-of.

**Want a portfolio as your homepage?** Choose the **Portfolio** preset in
**Settings > Style** - see [Pick a starting style](design.md#pick-a-starting-style).

## Build a landing page

Make one page for a book, a course, a launch or a product: a big opening,
then sections one after another.

1. Select **New post**, then **Template**, and choose **Landing page**.
2. Write the opening - the first section, a **Hero**. The `##` heading is
   its big heading; under it go an image, a line, and a link for the
   button. Two links on one line, like `[Try it](/demo) · [Learn more](/about)`,
   are a main button and a quieter one.
3. Go down the sections below it and replace their sample words: two
   **Image and text** sections, **Numbers**, **Testimonials**,
   **Pricing**, **FAQ** and a closing **Call to action**.
4. Delete the sections you don't need, or add more with **Insert** - see
   [Add sections](#add-sections).
5. Select **Preview** to see the page, then **Save**.

The template gives the page a wide width, a large space between sections,
and no title, byline or contents - the Hero's heading is the page's. Give
the page a title anyway: it names the page in **Posts and pages** and in
search results.

**Want a small tag above the heading?** On the first line under the Hero's
`Style:` line, write `Label: Free and open source`.

**Want a section narrower, or on a card?** Change its `Width:` or `Style:`
line - see [Width, style, heading, space and appear](sections-templates.md#width-style-heading-space-and-appear).

**Want the title back, or a different width?** Change **Title** or **Page
width** in **Post settings > Page**.

**Want it to be your homepage?** See [Choose the homepage](#choose-the-homepage).

## Add sections

A section is a part of a post or page with its own look - your prices,
kind words from clients, a row of numbers, a sign-up box. You can add
sections to any post or page.

1. Put your cursor where the section should go.
2. Select **Insert** on the toolbar, and choose a section from the
   **Sections** group. Or type `/` and the section's name, like
   `/pricing` or `/team`.
3. Fill in what it adds. Each item in a section is a `###` heading with a
   few lines under it.
4. Select **Preview** to see how it looks, then **Save**.

In your writing, a section starts with a `##` heading, with a line under
it saying what kind of section it is - for example `Section:
Testimonials`. The section runs until the next `##` heading. Under that
line, it has a line for each of its options, set to `Default` and shown
faded - change one to change how the section looks.

**Want ordinary writing after a section?** Start a new `##` heading.

**Want it wider, or set apart?** Change the `Width:` line to `Wide` or
`Full` to widen it, or the `Style:` line to `Card` to put it on a card.
See [Width, style, heading, space and appear](sections-templates.md#width-style-heading-space-and-appear).

**Does the `Section:` line show to readers?** No. It doesn't show on the
page, in feeds or in the newsletter.

**Building a whole page from sections?** Start from the **Landing page**
template - see [Build a landing page](#build-a-landing-page).

## Go to a section

Jump to any section of a long page without scrolling to find it.

1. Select **Go to** at the right end of the toolbar, just before
   **Insert**. On a phone, select the list icon in the bar above the
   keyboard. Or, at the start of an empty line, type `/go` and choose
   **Go to a section** - the same list opens where you're typing, and
   nothing is added to your writing.
2. Choose a section. The list has one row for each, in the order they're
   on the page - its kind, like **Columns**, then the text of its heading.
   The one your cursor is in has a checkmark.

Your cursor goes to the start of that section's heading, and the text
scrolls to it - in **Side by side**, the preview follows. It works in
**Focus** too, where the heading lands in the middle of the screen if
**Typewriter scrolling** is on.

**Using the keyboard?** Press **Enter** on **Go to**, then ↑ and ↓ to
move, **Enter** to go there, or **Esc** to close it.

**No sections yet?** The list says so, and **Insert a section…** opens
**Insert** at the sections you can add - see [Add sections](#add-sections).

**Don't see Go to?** With **None** chosen for **Toolbar** in
**Settings > Editor > Toolbar buttons**, there's no toolbar. Type `/go`
instead, or choose **Icons** or **Menus** to have it.

## Show a page as slides

Turn a talk into slides readers flip through, one at a time.

1. Select **New post**, then **Template**, and choose **Slides**. Or, on a
   page you've already written, open **Post settings > Page**, select
   **More** and switch on **Show as slides**.
2. Write each slide, with a line of only `---` between one slide and the
   next.
3. Select **Preview** to try it, then **Save**.

Readers see one slide at a time, filling the screen, with a counter,
arrow buttons and a **Full screen** button. They move on with the arrow
buttons or keys, or by swiping. **Back to the site**, in the corner, or
pressing **Esc**, leaves the slides.

**Only one slide?** A page needs at least one `---` line - two slides - to
show as slides. With fewer it stays an ordinary page.

## Keep a now page up to date

Show readers what you're up to these days, and be reminded to keep it
fresh.

1. Select **New post**, then **Template**, and choose **Now page**. Or, on
   any page, open **Post settings > Page**, select **More** and switch on
   **Remind me to update**.
2. Write what you're up to.
3. Select **Save**.

The page shows when it was last updated, in a box at the top. Three months
after, a note appears for you - and only you - saying how many days it's
been and asking whether it's time for an update. Save the page again to
clear it.

**Does it work on any page?** Yes - **Remind me to update** is a Post
setting, so any post or page can have it.

## Make a page for the menu or footer

Add an About, Contact or Privacy page to the menu at the top of your
site, or to its footer at the bottom.

1. Select **New post** and write the page.
2. Open **Post settings > Publishing**, and set **Where it appears** to
   **In the menu** or **In the footer**.
3. To show it under another page - as a dropdown in the menu, or in the
   same column of a footer laid out in columns - choose that page as its
   **Parent page**. Soloblog's own pages in that menu, like **Home** or
   **Blog**, are offered there too.
4. Switch **Published** on and select **Save**.

The page is now in your menu or footer, at a short URL like
`/about`. It shows only its title, cover image and text - no date, share
buttons or author box, as those suit posts more than pages.

**Is the URL you want taken?** A few names are Soloblog's own, like
`/contact` and `/search`. A page with one of those names stays at
`/post/` and its name, and **Post settings** says so under its URL.
Choose another URL there, or see
[URLs Soloblog uses](reference.md#urls-soloblog-uses).

**Want the date or share buttons on it after all?** Select them in **Post
settings > Page**, under **Show**.

**Want to change the order of the menu?** Open **Posts and pages** and
select **Pinned**. The **Header** list there is your menu, in order.
**Home** is in it as a **Site page** (and **Blog**, if a page of your
own is your [homepage](#choose-the-homepage)), so you can put them
anywhere among your pages. Each row has a **Move** button with **Move
up** and **Move down** (on a phone, under the **⋯** button). A menu
page can also be moved under the page above it, so it shows in that
page's dropdown. Nothing goes under a site page.

**Want to call Home something else?** On its row, select **Rename**,
type the label for the menu, and select **Save**. Every site page can be
renamed the same way. Leave it empty to go back to the page's own name,
in your visitors' language. Your own labels show as you typed them in
every language.

**Want Home out of the menu?** Select **Hide from menu** on its row. It
shows as **Hidden**, and **Show in menu** puts it back. Your site's name
still goes to the homepage.

**Want the same for the footer?** The **Footer** list below it works the
same way: **Add to footer**, **Move**, **Rename** and **Remove from
footer**. Home and Blog can go in the footer too. A page can be in the
menu, the footer, both or neither, with its own place and label in each.
They show only while **Pinned pages** is on, under **Footer** in
**Settings > Header and footer**. Archive, Tags and RSS in the footer's small print (the
**Archive, Tags and RSS links** switch there) stay as they are, but a
page you add to the footer isn't shown twice.

**Need a privacy policy or terms of use?** Start a new post from the
**Privacy policy** or **Terms** template. They're already outlined, and
already set to show in the footer. See
[Start a post from a template](writing.md#start-a-post-from-a-template).

## Choose the homepage

Your homepage is what people see first when they visit your site. It
starts as your latest posts. You can show a page of your own there
instead.

1. Make the page. It can be in your menu or footer, or unlisted, which
   keeps it out of the menu.
2. Open **Settings > Name and homepage**.
3. Under **Your homepage shows**, choose **One of your pages**.
4. Select **Pick** and choose the page.

The page is now the front of your site. Your posts move to `/blog`, and
your menu gets a **Blog** link to them. The page itself isn't listed
among your posts or in your feeds, and search finds it at your
homepage's URL - unlisted or not.

**Want more on the homepage than the page itself?** Under **It also
shows**, select what to add - its title, reactions, share buttons, a
newsletter sign-up box, comments and more.

**Keeping a knowledge base or a digital garden?** Choose **Your wiki**
instead - see [Build a wiki](#build-a-wiki).

**Want your latest posts back?** Choose **Your latest posts**.

## Add a link or a built-in page to the menu

Put one of Soloblog's built-in pages, a tag or a link to another site in
your menu or footer.

**To add a built-in page** (Archive, Tags, Search, Wiki, Links, Contact,
Newsletter or the RSS feed):

1. Open **Posts and pages** and select **Pinned**.
2. Select **Add to menu** above the **Header** list, or **Add to footer**
   above the **Footer** list, and choose the page. The list shows what
   each one is, and only the pages that aren't there yet and are turned
   on for your site: **Wiki** needs a wiki note, **Links** needs the links
   page switched on, **Contact** and **Newsletter** need to be set up.

The page is added at the end, as a **Site page**. Use **Move** to put it
elsewhere, **Rename** to change its label, and **Remove from menu** (or
**Remove from footer**) to take it out - it's then in the **Add** list
again.

**A page you added stopped working?** If you remove the last wiki note,
or switch the links page off, the page leaves the menu by itself. Its row
stays in **Pinned**, greyed out, and says why - for example **No wiki
notes yet**. It comes back when the page does.

**To add a link to another site, a tag or one of your own pages:**

1. Select **New post** and give it the title the menu should show.
2. Open **Post settings > Page**, select **More**, and type the URL in
   **Link to**. Or select **Pick** to choose one of your posts or built-in
   pages.
3. Open **Post settings > Publishing**, set **Where it appears** to **In
   the menu** or **In the footer**, switch **Published** on, and select
   **Save**.

Selecting it in the menu goes straight to that URL.

**Want a tag in the menu?** Use the tag's URL in **Link to**, like
`/tag/travel`.

**Which built-in pages are there?** `/archive` (every post, by month),
`/tags`, `/search`, `/contact`, `/newsletter`, `/links`, `/wiki` and the
members' pages - see
[Built-in pages and feeds](reference.md#built-in-pages-and-feeds).
**Pick** lists the ones that are turned on for your site. The ones above
can be added with **Add to menu**; the members' pages are only a link.

**Writing about someone else's page?** Start from the **Link post**
template, put the page's URL in **Link to**, and write your thoughts.
Your title links there, and a button under your text does too.

## Start a series

A series joins several posts that readers go through in order, like the
parts of a story or the lessons of a course.

1. In the first post, open **Post settings > Series**.
2. Type a **Name** for the series, and put `1` in **Order**.
3. In each of the other posts, do the same, with the same name and the
   next number.
4. Save each post.

Each post in the series now shows which part it is, like "Part 2 of 4",
with links to the others. The URL `/series/` followed by the series'
name lists every part, in order.

**Writing a tutorial?** Start each part from the **Docs / guide**
template. It puts the contents on the left and turns on **Series links**
under **Show**.

**Want a page that lists all the parts?** Add a **Contents** section to it -
**Insert > Sections > Contents**. It lists the series' published parts
in order, as links, and keeps up as parts are added or moved. It uses the
page's own series; to list another, put its name on the `Series:` line
under `Section: Contents`. See
[Contents](sections-templates.md#section-contents).

**Want to see all your series?** In **Posts and pages**, set **Type** to
**In a series**. It lists the posts that are in one.

## Build a wiki

A wiki is a set of notes that link to each other - a knowledge base, or a
digital garden - kept beside your blog.

1. Select **New post** and write a note.
2. Open **Post settings > Page**, select **More** and switch on **In the
   wiki**. Or start from the **Wiki / notes** template, which switches it
   on.
3. To link to another note, type its title between double square
   brackets, like `[[Their title]]`. As you type `[[` and a few letters,
   your posts and pages with those letters in their title are suggested:
   pick one with the ↑ and ↓ keys and **Enter**, or select it. **Esc**
   closes the list and keeps what you typed.
4. Select **Save**.

Each note lists the notes it links to, and the notes that link back to
it. The URL `/wiki` is your wiki's front page: notes to start from,
the latest edited, the most read, and every note from A to Z.

**Do notes show in the blog?** No, they live in the wiki. Tags, search,
the archive and feeds include them.

**Want a note readers start from?** Pin it - to the top of the homepage,
the menu or the footer, in **Post settings > Publishing** - and it shows
under **Start here** on the wiki's front page.

**Linked to a note you haven't written yet?** The link shows in red for
you, and as plain text for visitors, until you write that note.

**Where's the wiki's front page?** It's made for you at `/wiki` from
your notes - there's nothing to write. To put it in the menu, choose
**Wiki** under **Add to menu** - see
[Add a link or a built-in page to the menu](#add-a-link-or-a-built-in-page-to-the-menu).

**Want the wiki as your homepage?** In **Settings > Name and homepage**,
choose **Your wiki**.
