## Write and format a post

Write a post and publish it on your blog.

1. Select **New post**, the button at the top of the admin menu.
2. Type the title where it says **Title**, then write your post below it.
3. Use the toolbar above the text to format your writing - headings,
   bold, italic, lists, quotes and links. Point at any button to see its
   name and keyboard shortcut.
4. Select **Settings** - the gear icon beside **Save** - and switch
   **Published** on.
5. Select **Save**, or press **Ctrl+S**.

Your post is now on your blog. The first time you save, the post gets its
own URL, made from its title. The small dot beside **Save** is
green when everything is saved, and changes colour while you have changes
that aren't saved yet. Point at it to see what it means.

At the top of the editor, the name says whether it's a **Post** or a
**Page**, and a label beside it whether it's a **Draft**, **Published**
or **Scheduled**. Small labels under the name show who can read it and
any sections it has, and change as you change its settings. Select a
section's name to go to that section in the writing. When there are more
names than fit on one line, they're gathered under one label, such as
**7 sections**, that opens a list of them. If a page
has the same kind of section twice, select its name again to go to the
next one.

In the writing, a section's `Section:` line has a coloured band, and
blocks such as `Buttons:` and `Pull quote:` a grey one, so you can tell
them from your text.

**Want to check it first?** Select **Preview**, above the toolbar, or
press **Ctrl+Alt+P**. You see the post exactly as readers will. Select
**Write**, or press **Esc**, to go back to writing.

**Not ready to publish?** Leave **Published** off and save. The post is
a draft, which only you can see. To show a draft to someone before it's
published, open **Settings**: at the top is a link anyone can open
without logging in. Select **Copy** and send it to them.

**The page closed before you saved?** Open the post again, and the
writing you hadn't saved is offered back to you.

The line along the bottom of the editor counts your words and how long
the post takes to read. To aim for a length, set a **Word goal** in
**Settings > Details**, and a ring fills as you write.

## Add images and galleries

Put an image in a post, or several images together as a gallery.

1. Put your cursor where the image should go.
2. Paste or drop the image there. Or select the image button on the
   toolbar, and pick an image you've already uploaded or upload a new
   one.
3. Describe the image for people who can't see it: open **Media** in the
   admin menu, select the image and fill in **Alt text**. Screen readers
   read it aloud, and it shows if the image can't load.

The image is added to your **Media** library and shows in the post. Big
images are made a sensible size for the web, and any location your
camera saved in the photo is removed.

**To add a gallery:** select **Gallery** on the toolbar and pick the
images. Then choose how they're laid out:
- **Grid** - even squares
- **Masonry** - columns, each image at its own height
- **Justified** - rows of equal height, edge to edge
- **Slider** - one image at a time, with arrows
- **Filmstrip** - a row you scroll sideways

Readers can open any image in a gallery full size, move between the
images with the arrows or by swiping, and zoom in.

**Forgot some alt text?** After you save, the line beside **Save** says
how many images have none. To find every image without it, open
**Media** and set **Kind** to **No alt text**.

**Want to crop or rotate an image?** Select it in **Media**. **Crop to**,
**Rotate** and **File name** are there, and every post that uses the
image shows the change.

## Add video, audio and PDFs

Play your own video or audio in a post, or show a PDF.

1. Open **Media** in the admin menu and upload the file. It can be audio
   (mp3, m4a, ogg, wav, flac), video (mp4, webm, mov) or a PDF.
2. Select **Copy URL** on the file. This copies its URL.
3. In your post, paste the URL on a line of its own.

Audio and video become a player readers can press play on. A PDF shows
inside the post, with an **Open the PDF** link under it for readers on a
phone.

**Is the video on YouTube or Vimeo?** Paste its link from there instead -
see [Embed from other sites](#embed-from-other-sites).

**Want music or a sound to play through a whole page?** In the editor,
open **Settings**, then **Page**, and choose a **Background sound**.
Readers get a play button; it never starts by itself.

## Embed from other sites

Show a YouTube video, a map, a social media post or a form inside your
post.

1. On the other site, copy the link to the video, post, map or form.
2. In your post, paste the link on a line of its own.

It becomes a card in your post. The video, map or form loads when a
reader selects the card. Until then, nothing is loaded from the other
site.

This works with YouTube, Vimeo, Spotify, Mastodon, X, Facebook,
Instagram, TikTok, OpenStreetMap, Google Maps, and forms from Tally,
Typeform, Google Forms, Jotform, Microsoft Forms and Airtable.

**Want an ordinary link instead of a card?** Put the link inside a
sentence, and it stays a link.

## Use Insert and the slash menu

Find anything you can add to a post - a table, a callout box, a section
of a page - without hunting through the toolbar.

1. Select **Insert** at the right end of the toolbar. Or, at the start of
   an empty line, type `/` - this opens the same list, called the slash
   menu.
2. Type a few letters to narrow the list: `gal` finds Gallery, `tab`
   finds Table.
3. Pick one with the ↑ and ↓ keys and press **Enter**, or select it.

It's added where your cursor is. The three you used most recently are at
the top of the list.

**Changing how a section, a gallery or a box looks?** Put your cursor
anywhere in it and open **Insert** or type `/` - on a phone, select
**+**. Its options come first, and the one it uses now says **In use**:

- **Any section:** its width (**Column**, **Wide** or **Full**) and style
  (**Card**, and **Cover** on a Hero, Call to action or Picture and text).
- **Portfolio:** how it's arranged (**Show as**) and a tag whose posts it
  adds. **Latest posts:** how many (**Count**) and which tag.
  **Contents:** which series. **Photo story:** how its first paragraph,
  images and short lines are arranged. **Hero:** also **Window**.
- **Gallery:** its style, and for a slider, whether it moves by itself.
- **Callout:** Note, Tip, Warning or Danger. **Collapsible:** whether it
  starts open.

Choosing one writes the line for you. Each also has **Default** first -
the same as no line, so it follows the site - and a section you add
comes with every line written, set to `Default` and shown faded.

**Typed a value the page can't use?** Like `Width: Tall`, or a kind of
section that doesn't exist. (`Tag: Default` too, while your site has a
tag called Default - it's a reserved word.) It's underlined in red, with a line under it
saying what it can be. Readers never see either.

**Other words work too:** `/h2` finds Heading, `/todo` finds Checklist,
and `/youtube` finds Video or embed.

**Typed a `/` you meant to keep?** Press **Esc**. The list closes and the
`/` stays.

**Use something often?** You can give it its own button on the toolbar,
and choose the order of the buttons, in **Settings > Editor > Toolbar
buttons**.

**Not sure what something does?** Select the **?** button beside
**Save**, press **Ctrl+/**, or type `/help`. The editor's help opens
beside your writing, with an example of everything you can add and every
keyboard shortcut.

**Looking for a section you wrote?** **Go to**, just before **Insert**,
lists them and takes you to one - see
[Go to a section](pages.md#go-to-a-section).

## Write in Focus

Fill the screen with your writing and nothing else, so nothing distracts
you.

1. Select **Focus**, above the toolbar, or press **Ctrl+Shift+F**.
2. Write. Once you start typing, the bars and the toolbar slide out of
   the way.
3. To come back, press **Esc**, or select **Leave Focus** - the ✕ at the
   top of the screen.

To bring the bars back for a moment, move the mouse to the top of the
screen. On a phone, select the small arrow at the top middle of the
screen, or scroll up.

**Want to write for a set time or a set number of words?** The bar at
the top has a **Writing session** timer: 25 or 50 minutes, 500 or 1,000
words, or your own target. A thin line along the top fills as you go.

**Text too small or too big?** **A−** and **A+**, in the same bar, make
it smaller or bigger.

**On a wide screen,** **Side by side** shows your writing and the preview
next to each other. On a computer, Focus also keeps the line you're
writing in the middle of the screen and fades the rest of the page. You
can turn each of these on or off in **Settings > Editor > Focus**.

## Schedule a post

Write a post now, and have it published by itself at a time you choose.

1. In the editor, open **Settings** (the gear icon), then **Publishing**.
2. Set **Publish at** to the day and time. **Published** switches on by
   itself.
3. Select **Save**.

The post shows as **Scheduled** in **Posts and pages**, and on the
**Calendar** there. It's published at the time you set, in your site's
time zone.

**Change your mind?** To publish it now, clear **Publish at** and save.
To keep it as a draft, switch **Published** off and save.

**Wrong time zone?** Change it in **Settings > Language and time**.

## Start a post from a template

A template is a page to start from, with its sections, sample words and
settings already in place - a recipe, a price list, a resume, an FAQ.
Choose one, and you write over the sample words instead of starting from
nothing.

1. Select **New post**.
2. Select **Template** - the label with ▾ under the title, at the top of
   the editor.
3. Choose a template. Type to find one: the ones you used most recently
   are under **Recent**, the ones you saved under **Custom**, and the ones
   that come with Soloblog under **Built-in**. **Blank** starts an empty
   post.
4. Write over the sample words, then save as usual.

   Some templates start with sample images. Until you replace one, your
   readers see the page without it; you see a dashed box saying **Image
   not found - replace it**, where it will go.

Your post opens with the template's sections, words and settings in
place. If you'd already written something, you're asked first whether to
leave it.

Soloblog comes with a template for each kind of page - a recipe, a
resume, a landing page, a wiki note, slides, a link post, a coming-soon
page and more - plus a journal page, a how-to, a **Privacy policy** and
**Terms**. What each one has is in
[Sections and templates](sections-templates.md#templates).

**Left the sample words in?** They're only there to show where yours
go. Replace them before you publish.

**Want to change a template afterwards?** The page is yours: change,
add or delete anything. It keeps no link to the template, so what you
change doesn't change the template.

**Want every new post to start from one?** See
[Choose what new posts start from](#choose-what-new-posts-start-from).

See also: [Apply a template to a page](#apply-a-template-to-a-page) ·

## Choose what new posts start from

Have every new post open from a template of your choice, so you don't
choose each time.

1. Open **Settings > Editor** and go to **Templates**.
2. Beside **New posts start from**, choose a template. Type to find one.
3. Select **Save**.

**New post** now opens a post with that template's sections, words and
settings in place, and no list of templates. **Blank** - the choice to
start with - opens an empty post.

**Want another template for one post?** Select **Template** under the
title in the editor and choose it - see
[Start a post from a template](#start-a-post-from-a-template).

**Deleted the template you chose?** New posts start blank again.

## Apply a template to a page

Add a template to a page you've already written - say, a Pricing section
and the settings of a landing page - without starting again.

1. Open the post or page in the editor.
2. Open **Settings** - the gear icon beside **Save** - then **Template**.
3. Select **Apply a template**.
4. Choose a template. Type to find one.

The template's words are added at the end of the page, with all its
sections, and its settings are used. Nothing you'd written is touched.
Beside **Save**, a line says what happened - for example "Recipe applied ·
3 settings changed" - with **Undo** beside it. On a phone, the line is a
bar at the bottom of the screen. Nothing is saved until you select
**Save**.

**What happens to the page's settings?** The template's **Post settings**
replace the page's own - its title, page width, cover image style,
contents, folding, spacing, side panels, what **Show** ticks, and the
settings under **More**. A setting the template doesn't change goes back
to following the site. Open **Page** to see what's now **Custom**. The
page's summary, tags and cover image are filled in from the template only
if they're empty. What you've chosen about publishing - published or not,
the date, the URL, who can read it, the series - stays as it was.

**Applied two?** Both templates' sections are on the page, and the second
one's settings win.

**Already have that section?** You get another. Every section of the
template is added, even one the page already has.

**Applied the wrong one?** Select **Undo** before you change anything
else. It puts back the text and every setting. Later, **Discard** - the
✕ beside **Save** - takes the page back to how it was last saved.

## Save a page as a template

Keep a page's words and settings as a template of your own, for the things
you write in the same shape again and again.

1. Open a page with the text and settings you want to reuse.
2. Open **Settings** - the gear icon - then **Template**.
3. Under **Save as a template**, type a name. It starts as the page's
   title.
4. Select the **Save** button beside the name.

A line under it says it's saved. The template keeps the page's words, with
its sections as written, its **Post settings** - everything under **Page**,
including **More** - and its tags, summary and cover image. It leaves out
what's about this one page: whether it's published, its date, its URL,
who can read it and its series.

Your template is listed under **Custom**, with a **Custom** label, when you
start a post and when you apply a template.

**Saved it under a name you've used?** It replaces the earlier template of
that name.

**Want to remove one of your templates?** They're listed in **Settings >
Editor > Templates**, each with a button to remove it. Pages made from it
stay as they are.

## Bring back an earlier version

Each time you save, Soloblog keeps the version from before. You can look
at what a post said earlier, and bring that version back.

1. In the editor, open **Settings** (the gear icon), then **History**.
2. Select **Compare** on a version to see what changed in it - red was
   taken out, green was added.
3. Select **Restore** on the version you want back. It replaces the text
   in the editor.
4. Select **Save** to keep it.

Nothing changes until you save, so you can restore a version to look at
it, and leave it if it's not the one. Each post keeps its last 50 saved
versions.

**Restored the wrong one?** Select **Discard** - the ✕ beside **Save** -
to go back to the post as it was last saved. Once the text has changed,
it asks first.

**Looking for a post you moved to the trash?** It's in
**Posts and pages > Trash**, where you can restore it.

## Write on a phone

Write and publish from your phone, with the same editor.

1. Open your site's admin in your phone's browser. Open the admin menu
   with the menu button at the top left, and select **New post**.
2. Write. The toolbar sits just above the keyboard; swipe along it to
   see more buttons.
3. For everything else, select **+** at the end of the toolbar, or type
   `/` at the start of a line.
4. Select **Save**.

**Settings**, **Preview** and **Help** are icons beside **Save**. If they
don't all fit on your screen, the rest are under **⋯**.

**Prefer a different toolbar on your phone?** In **Settings > Editor**,
under **On a phone**, choose **Your buttons** (a row you swipe along),
**Sections** (one button for each group, each opening a grid of
buttons) or **None** (type `/` instead).

**Writing in another app?** iA Writer, Ulysses and other writing apps can
publish straight to your site - see
[Connect writing apps and AI assistants](run.md#connect-writing-apps-and-ai-assistants).
